Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit for your consideration the enclosed manuscript, **"Investment Gestation Lags, Intangible Capital, and the Measurement of Capital and Productivity in 39 Economies"**, for publication in *Structural Change and Economic Dynamics*.

The paper examines a measurement question that sits squarely within the journal's scope: how the structural transformation of investment — the secular shift from plant and machinery toward software, R&D, and other long-gestation intangible assets — distorts the capital stocks and productivity residuals produced by standard national-accounts conventions. We jointly estimate a time-varying investment-to-output lag μ(t) and a country-specific intangible-capital share β for 39 OECD and middle-income economies over 1970–2019, using a tempo–quantum decomposition transplanted from formal demography. The corrections narrow the gap between production-side and wealth-side national accounts — surviving a strict temporal holdout on the wealth series (fitted on 1995–2014 and evaluated against 2015–2019, the joint model attains a smaller residual than the production-only alternative in 23 of 39 countries) — revise measured capital by a median −4.3 %, and raise log-level TFP and the implied labour share by a median 1.7 percentage points each, with the largest effects precisely where the investment mix has shifted most.

I believe this work is well-suited to *Structural Change and Economic Dynamics* for three reasons.

First, the paper's subject is structural change itself: it quantifies how a documented shift in the composition of capital formation propagates through the measurement system on which the analysis of structural and technological change depends. Readers of the journal working on productivity dynamics, intangible-driven growth, or the changing configuration of national accounts will find directly usable estimates.

Second, the analysis contributes econometric tools designed for the study of structural dynamics. A time-varying gestation lag μ(t) is identified jointly with the intangible share β by a flow–stock consistency constraint; the "Relational PIM" adapts Brass relational models to capital accounting; and an observable, parameter-free tempo proxy M_obs is constructed from OECD asset-composition data, so the core correction can be replicated at negligible cost by any statistical agency.

Third, the reporting is deliberately conservative: the out-of-sample improvement of the tempo correction (median MAPE 4.60 % → 3.99 %) is reported together with its non-significant Wilcoxon test, and robustness is documented extensively in the Supporting Information — Monte Carlo identification checks, depreciation-sensitivity, R&D-scaling sensitivity, and historical-episode decompositions.
